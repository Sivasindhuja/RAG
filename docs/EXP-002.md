# EXP-002 — Structure-Aware Chunking Experiment

Status: **completed; rejected as the production default.**
Completed: 2026-11-14.
Runner: `experiments/exp002.py`.
Artifacts: `experiments/results/exp002/`.
Companion: `docs/ENGINEERING_LOG.md` (EXP-002 entry), `docs/EXP-001.md`.

---

## 1. Objective

Test whether clause-anchored **structure-aware chunking** improves evidence-level
retrieval over the accepted **fixed-character** baseline, while holding every
other variable constant.

Only the chunking strategy may change.

---

## 2. Hypothesis

Clause-anchored chunks should keep legally meaningful units intact and improve
retrieval, especially for single-clause questions, without hurting compound
multi-clause questions.

This hypothesis was **not supported** by the results.

## 6. Failure analysis

### 6.1 EXP001-Q08 — Recall@5 0.0 (baseline 0.3333)

- Question: *What authorisations or licenses are required to establish an Indian
  Satellite System?*
- Required: `3.4.1`, `3.4.2`, `3.4.3`.
- Retrieved top-5: parent `3.4`, then `3.5`, `3.6.9`, `4.4.3`, and an
  unsectioned page-12 body chunk.
- Missing: all three evidence units (`Q08-E01…E03`).

**Interpretation.** The retriever found the parent clause `3.4` ("LICENSES
REQUIRED") but not its three child clauses. Because the child clauses are
separate chunks, one parent hit does not cover any required evidence. The
fixed-character baseline's chunks crossed those child boundaries and happened to
cover one of them.

### 6.2 EXP001-Q13 — Recall@5 0.25 (baseline 1.0)

- Question: *What are the categories of domestic satellite communication
  services?*
- Required: `4.3.1`, `4.3.2`, `4.3.3`, `4.3.4`.
- Retrieved top-5: `4.1`, `4.3`, `4.3.4`, `4.2`, `4.2`.
- Covered: only `4.3.4` (E04). Missing: `4.3.1`, `4.3.2`, `4.3.3`.

**Interpretation.** The retriever found the parent classification `4.3` and the
last child `4.3.4`, but not the sibling child clauses that actually enumerate
the categories. Clause-level splitting fragmented one conceptual answer across
several low-signal chunks.

### 6.3 EXP001-Q05 — Recall@5 1.0 (baseline 0.0) — improvement

- Question: *Who is responsible for obtaining licenses to offer services in a
  territory?*
- Required: `2.5.6`.
- Rank 1 hit `2.5.6` (203 overlap chars).

**Interpretation.** This is the case structure-aware chunking was meant to win:
the answer is a single clause that fixed-character chunks had missed.

### 6.4 Why precision and MRR fell

- 16 chunks are under 100 characters, so some embeddings carry weak signal.
- Sibling clauses compete with each other; a generic parent chunk or a
  neighbouring clause often ranks above the exact child that the question needs.
- Splitting a conceptual answer into many small chunks increases the chance that
  a non-answer chunk ranks first (lower MRR) and that the top-5 contains fewer
  distinct relevant chunks (lower precision).

---

## 7. Decision

**Rejected as the production default for this configuration.**

Rationale:

- Precision@5 fell by 0.0666 and MRR@5 by 0.1700; recall was effectively flat
  (−0.0056).
- The regressions are concentrated in exactly the compound, multi-clause
  questions the application must handle well (Q08, Q13).
- The one clear improvement (Q05) does not offset the loss of parent/child
  coverage elsewhere.
- Aggregate metrics alone are not decisive on 15 questions; the failure
  inspection confirms the cause.

**Retained for:** metadata-rich clause baseline and the foundation of a future
controlled **parent-context / sibling-expansion** experiment. That experiment has
not been run and must not be assumed to succeed.

  semantic chunking or generation was introduced.

- The unfavourable result is reported as-is.
